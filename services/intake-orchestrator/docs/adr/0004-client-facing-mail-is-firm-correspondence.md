# Client-facing mail is firm correspondence, not product notification

A potential client's first contact with a firm arrived as a bare LLM question from "LegalGate Agenda", on a fresh thread, with no salutation or signature. Everything sent to a potential client now carries the Firm Voice instead: the firm's name as sender, a salutation, a signature from the firm's consultation team, and no mention of LegalGate. Mail to lawyers and firm staff keeps LegalGate branding, because they are the customer and know what LegalGate is.

## Consequences

Two things here look like inconsistencies and are not, so they should not be "fixed":

**Conversational mail is plaintext; transactional mail is HTML.** The scheduled and rescheduled notices stay as rendered HTML cards because they are receipts and a receipt should look like one. Diagnostics questions and the Non-Engagement Notice are plaintext, because a branded card asking a stranger for their ID number reads as automation no matter how well designed it is. Consistency across the two was rejected as a goal — sounding like a person was the goal.

**The client receipt carries the firm's name as text, and nothing else of anyone's brand.** The scheduled-consultation card a potential client receives keeps its layout, colors and structure, but the LegalGate wordmark, the pixel logo and the "LEGALGATE · INTAKE AUTOMÁTICO" footer are gone: the header reads the firm's name, resolved from the tenant's organization display name and falling back to the global sender name, the same name the message is sent from. The footer keeps only the line inviting a reply — the firm is named once, at the top, the way letterhead works. That fallback is the one place client-facing mail may still say "LegalGate": a card with an empty header is worse than one carrying the same name the message is sent from, and a tenant with no display name has no other name to show. A signature has no such problem — it is omitted instead. A firm's own logo and brand colors are deliberately not there — per-tenant assets are a separate piece of work, and a neutral card in the firm's name is already honest, while a LegalGate-branded one is not. The lawyer-facing card keeps LegalGate branding, because firm staff are the customer.

**The Consultation Thread is anchored flat, not chained.** Every message LegalGate sends a potential client about a Consultation points at that client's original email rather than at the previous message in the exchange. Mail clients group on a shared root, so a flat anchor and a true chain land in the same conversation; the chain would have cost persisted message identifiers on two more tables to buy correct nesting in a handful of readers.

The thread is the conversation with the potential client, so only client-facing mail carries `In-Reply-To` and `References`. Notices to lawyers and firm staff are about the Consultation but not part of that conversation, and anchoring them to a stranger's Message-ID would fold firm mail into the client's thread. Every outbound message still carries its own `Message-ID`, on the domain it was sent from — that is the message's identity, not a claim about a thread. The anchor lives on the Consultation and is read at send time, so the outbox carries no message identifiers; when it is missing the mail sends unthreaded, because threading is an enhancement and never a reason not to deliver.

Signatures name the firm's consultation team and never a lawyer. During Diagnostics no lawyer has been assigned and nobody has read the file, so signing with a person's name would be a claim the firm cannot stand behind.

**The Non-Engagement Notice body is inserted verbatim.** The notice is tenant-configurable free text, and a firm that has written a complete letter into that field must not end up with two greetings. The template supplies the salutation, the signature and the not-legal-advice line and touches nothing in between — no rewording, nothing stripped but the whitespace around the body. The console field help says what the envelope adds, so a firm writing the field knows not to write its own greeting and sign-off. The built-in default notice is a body like any other and carries no salutation or sign-off of its own; it does state that no attorney-client relationship was formed, which the envelope's disclaimer — a statement about the message, not about the matter — does not say for it.

Sounding human is bounded by what LegalGate is allowed to assert. The Acknowledgment repeats the potential client's own words back and stops there. The template — not the model — supplies every fixed sentence, so a bad generation cannot put an inappropriate line into a law firm's first contact with a stranger.

**The Acknowledgment degrades; it never fails a round.** It travels as its own field on the diagnostics response rather than folded into the question, so it can be dropped without touching what the firm asked. Blank, or longer than fifteen words, and it is replaced by the bare receipt "Recibimos su mensaje." — a restatement that grew past a line has stopped repeating the potential client and started summarizing their matter. A degraded email is fine; a failed round is not, so unlike the usability check on the verdict this one never sends the response back for a retry. The diagnostics system prompt version is bumped alongside the wording it asks for, so generations before and after are distinguishable when auditing a matter; it is read live on every call and so reaches sessions already in flight, while the per-session snapshot holds the tenant's own Diagnostics Prompt and is unaffected. That prompt outranks the tone instructions in the system prompt, and a test asserts it still travels verbatim: a warmer prompt is exactly the kind of change that quietly costs a firm the information it asked for.
